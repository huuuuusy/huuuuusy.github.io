<h2>Shiyu Hu <span class="intro-name-zh" lang="zh-CN">(胡世宇)</span></h2>
<p>AI is moving from answering single questions to carrying out sustained tasks in open environments: agents act over long horizons, video models and world models follow scenes that unfold over minutes or hours, and AI now works alongside people in classrooms and workplaces. These systems often get each step right yet fail to <strong>keep the right state</strong>: which target they are following, how long a description stays true, which evidence still counts, and where the person they work with stands. My research studies <strong>the dynamic states of humans and AI in open environments</strong>, asking what a system should keep, update, or leave open as conditions change, time passes, and people take part. Using human performance and human judgment as reference, I evaluate where this state holds and breaks, model systems that keep it, and apply them to UAV vision, video understanding, and education.</p>
